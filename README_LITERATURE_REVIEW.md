# LegitZK literature review and novelty assessment

This review began with the structured-contract experiment. The current [PDF Studio](README.md) also uses adaptive raster commitments and owner-attested field proofs. The [raster research notes](docs/pdf-studio-research.md) cover the closer document-redaction precedents. The table contains the references from the original scoping review.

**Updated 18 November 2026.** This focused scoping review used publicly indexed academic work, primary standards, vendor documentation, and original implementations. It covers relevant work published from 2022 through November 2026 as well as earlier technical foundations. Search themes included ZK redaction, signed commitments, anonymous credentials, selective disclosure, mandatory fields, recipient binding, PDF glyph leakage and compliance predicates. It does not establish exhaustive prior-art coverage.

## Proof and authenticated-data alternatives

Circom compiles arithmetic circuits and snarkjs supports proving backends including Groth16. Noir offers a circuit language with separate proving backends. They are useful when the policy expands beyond one bounded comparison. The current implementation uses libsodium Ristretto primitives and fixed-size Schnorr OR ranges, avoiding toolchain setup and trusted setup for this narrow predicate. Bulletproofs offer a more compact setup-free range-proof design and are a candidate follow-up baseline. [Circom](https://github.com/iden3/circom) · [snarkjs](https://github.com/iden3/snarkjs) · [Noir](https://noir-lang.org/docs/) · [libsodium](https://doc.libsodium.org/advanced/point-arithmetic/ristretto) · [Bulletproofs](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/1066)

Merkle commitments compress signed inventories into a root and can avoid publishing an entire digest list. The earlier structured contract has five fixed hashed fields and one algebraic amount commitment, so an explicitly signed inventory is simpler; it costs space linear in the schema. Signatures and all canonical-schema checks occur outside the range proof. The range proof is bound to the signed manifest, request, openings and exact rendered bytes through the public transcript.

## Research claim and experimental boundary

For the structured-contract experiment, the hypothesis is that **binding a signed structured contract, verifier-controlled policy and request, private threshold, disclosed openings, and canonical public-fields PDF to one presentation detects evidence substitution that survives a predicate-only control**. Evaluate this on controlled synthetic cases with verifier state and PDF rendering held fixed. The recipient-transplant experiment provides evidence for one missing-binding failure. Existing credential and document systems substantially overlap the mechanism. A product workflow and a reproducible failure-mode study are the current contribution. Establishing a cryptographic novelty claim would require systematic citation chaining and a comparison with implementations of the closest compositions.

The review does not establish pixel-level equivalence, arbitrary-PDF redaction safety, issuer truthfulness, legal compliance, or generality outside the fixed schema. For the current Studio, the measured contribution is fine-grid coverage with fewer authenticated tiles than the fixed-8 comparator on the included layouts. The field proof is an additional disclosure workflow and remains dependent on owner attestation. See [Protocol and threat model](docs/protocol.md) and [Experimental results](docs/paper.md).
